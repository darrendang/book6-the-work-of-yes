## Canonical distinction — search operator versus decision standard

The strongest Book 6 distinction from the HBR source is:

> **“Yes, and” is a search operator.**

> **“Earn the decision” is a decision standard.**

“Yes, and” is useful because it resists premature closure. It keeps the possibility space open long enough to ask what else may be possible and what constructive action may be available.

Book 6 asks the subsequent and broader question: after widening the possibility space, has enough work been done to justify continuing, changing course, stopping, saying yes, saying no, or deliberately remaining unresolved?

Therefore:

> **Possibility thinking is necessary, but it is not decision-making.**

## Canonical explanatory movement

For this distinction, the following movement is useful:

**OPEN → EXPLORE → JUDGE → DECIDE**

HBR’s “yes, and” belongs primarily to **OPEN / EXPLORE**.

Book 6’s **Earn the decision** governs the fuller movement, especially the transition from possibility into judgment and decision.

This is an explanatory lens, not a locked chapter architecture or a new formal framework.

## Relationship to the Avoidance Principle

The HBR source reinforces one side of Book 6’s existing tension.

“Yes, and” can prevent an **easy no** from closing the search space before the work of possibility has been done.

But possibility-opening is not sufficient by itself. A person can also keep generating possibilities after enough evidence exists to decide.

This produces a useful extension of the Book 6 failure analysis:

- **premature no** can avoid the work of possibility;
- **premature yes** can avoid the work of judgment;
- **perpetual possibility** can avoid the work of deciding.

The first two remain forms of premature certainty. The third is a different avoidance pattern: refusing closure after sufficient inquiry because continued exploration is easier than commitment.

Treat **perpetual possibility** as a canonical Book 6 failure mode to test against future evidence, not as a universal diagnosis.

## Canonical development question

> **When does possibility-opening stop helping and start postponing judgment?**

This question should be used to test future stories involving persistence, ideation, experimentation, optionality, analysis, and delayed commitment.

**Some dots are discovered. Some dots are created.**

Action can create experience. Experience can create new evidence. New evidence can change perspective. Perspective can change the next decision. The decision then creates the next dots.
