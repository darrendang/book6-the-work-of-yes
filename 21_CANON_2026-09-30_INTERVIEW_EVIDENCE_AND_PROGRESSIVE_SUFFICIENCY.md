## Existing spine preserved

> **What does someone need to see—or understand—before they have earned enough confidence to decide?**

> **Earn the decision.**

> **The easy no avoids the work of possibility.**

> **The easy yes avoids the work of judgment.**

> **Do not use a decision to avoid the work the decision requires.**

The interview sweep strengthens this spine rather than replacing it.

## Canonical finding 1 — experience is evidence, not a script

Past decisions can create useful context, pattern recognition, warning signs, and practical knowledge.

They do not automatically determine the next answer.

> **A prior decision can create a map without becoming a template.**

Experience should improve the quality of the next inquiry without silently taking ownership of the next decision.

## Canonical finding 2 — general expertise and situated knowledge are different

A person may know more about a class of decisions while another person knows more about the particular situation, relationship, environment, or consequence being decided.

Responsible judgment must distinguish those kinds of knowledge rather than treating one as automatically superior.

Adopted inquiry:

> **Who understands the class of decision better, and who understands this particular decision better?**

This does not eliminate legitimate authority, fiduciary duty, safety responsibility, or other genuine guardrails.

## Canonical finding 3 — authority does not imply informational completeness

The person who owns the decision does not necessarily possess all relevant evidence.

> **Decision authority does not imply informational completeness.**

A decision-maker may need information, expertise, perspective, challenge, and implementation knowledge held by other people.

> **The work of earning a decision can be distributed even when final decision authority is not.**

Distributed inquiry does not erase accountability.

## Canonical finding 4 — demonstrated judgment can change delegated latitude

Within an appropriate organizational, family, or developmental context, repeated relevant behavior can justify giving someone greater latitude to decide.

This develops the existing distinction between intrinsic human agency and delegated responsibility.

Greater latitude should remain bounded by the actual domain, stakes, consequences, and oversight obligations.

Trust is evidence only where the behavior supporting it remains relevant.

## Canonical finding 5 — confidence can be earned incrementally

Some decisions do not require confidence in an entire future state before any action is taken.

A bounded step may generate evidence that did not exist beforehand.

> **Confidence can be earned incrementally.**

This is not permission for commitment creep.

A justified next step earns only that step.

The next commitment must be reconsidered when the evidence, stakes, ownership, reversibility, or consequences materially change.

## Canonical finding 6 — method and objective are not the same

Different responsible people may meet the same legitimate objective through different methods.

A method that works for one person, team, child, organization, or decision environment should not automatically be converted into the universal responsible method.

This strengthens the existing control:

> **Different decisions require different work.**

It also protects Book 6 from becoming a single mandatory decision algorithm.

## Canonical finding 7 — constraints should shape inquiry before ending it

A genuine constraint may establish a boundary without establishing the entire answer.

The question is whether the constraint is:

- a true guardrail that closes a path;
- a requirement that must be satisfied;
- an assumption about implementation;
- or accumulated friction that has been mistaken for necessity.

Adopted inquiry:

> **Is the constraint telling me no—or telling me what the solution must respect?**

A real legal, ethical, safety, fiduciary, financial, or other non-negotiable guardrail remains binding.

## Canonical finding 8 — stopping is a new decision

Entering a commitment and deciding whether to continue are separate decisions.

A later stop can be earned even when the original commitment was reasonable.

Stopping is not automatically failure, just as persistence is not automatically courage.

The later decision should be judged using the later evidence without rewriting what was knowable at the original decision point.

## Adopted person-and-decision inquiry

The interview evidence adds a second dimension to the governing question.

Ask not only what must be understood about the choice, but also:

> **What must I understand about the person making the choice—not just the choice itself?**

Relevant factors may include situated knowledge, demonstrated judgment, values, maturity, consequence ownership, decision authority, and the evidence accumulated through prior behavior.

This is an inquiry lens, not a test of personal worth.

## Evidence significance

Before this sweep, several Book 6 strands—especially reciprocal trust, decision authority, negotiated sufficiency, and accumulated evidence—were conceptually strong but dependent on a narrow source base.

The interview sweep supplies independent source families across professional, mentor, colleague, spouse, parent-child, and sibling contexts.

The recurrence is sufficient to treat the abstractions above as Book 6 development canon.

This does not make every underlying story publication-ready or independently corroborated in all factual details.

## Remaining contradiction targets

The expanded evidence base makes future mining more selective.

Priority counterexamples now include:

- weak process / favorable outcome;
- a bounded experiment that creates harmful commitment creep;
- “we cannot know until we act” used to rationalize reckless action;
- accumulated trust that becomes stale or misleading;
- delegated latitude that should have been narrowed;
- experience that legitimately should control because a true guardrail is present.

These are higher-value tests than simply collecting more confirming stories.
