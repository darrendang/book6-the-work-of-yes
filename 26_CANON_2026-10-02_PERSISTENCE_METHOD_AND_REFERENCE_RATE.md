## Existing spine preserved

> **What does someone need to see—or understand—before they have earned enough confidence to decide?**

> **Earn the decision.**

> **The easy no avoids the work of possibility.**

> **The easy yes avoids the work of judgment.**

> **Do not use a decision to avoid the work the decision requires.**

This refinement does not privilege persistence over stopping. It sharpens the question of what persistence should remain attached to and how evidence should be interpreted before deciding whether to continue.

## Canonical finding 1 — persistence is not fidelity to the current method

A decision-maker may remain committed to an objective while changing or abandoning the method currently being used to pursue it.

> **Persistence is not fidelity to the current method.**

When new evidence shows that the current method is undermining the objective, responsible persistence may require subtraction, redesign, help, reallocation, or stopping one path in order to preserve another.

Conversely, changing methods does not prove that the objective itself remains warranted. Both the objective and the method remain open to judgment.

Adopted inquiry:

> **What am I actually trying to preserve: the objective, the method, the commitment, or my identity as someone who finishes?**

## Canonical finding 2 — prior commitment is not new evidence

A prior promise, investment, declaration, or identity can create a reason to examine continuation carefully. It does not, by itself, supply fresh evidence that continuation remains warranted.

> **A prior commitment is not, by itself, evidence that continuing remains warranted.**

This strengthens the existing tension:

**persistence ↔ letting go**

Persistence may reflect courage, discipline, or warranted confidence. It may also reflect pride, sunk cost, identity protection, habit, or reluctance to revise a prior story.

Adopted inquiry:

> **Am I continuing because current evidence supports the commitment—or because stopping would force me to revise the story I tell about myself?**

This is not an argument for abandoning commitments whenever continuation becomes difficult. Difficulty can be fully consistent with a well-earned decision.

## Canonical finding 3 — evidence requires a relevant reference rate or threshold

A sequence of negative results cannot be interpreted responsibly without knowing what rate of success the decision actually requires.

> **Evidence has meaning only in relation to the result the decision actually requires.**

Repeated rejection, failure, or non-conversion may be evidence that a strategy is failing. It may also be an expected feature of a strategy whose required success rate is low enough for the model to remain viable.

The opposite is also true: occasional success does not necessarily validate a strategy if the decision requires a materially higher success rate, acceptable downside, or different quality of result.

Adopted inquiries:

> **What result does this strategy actually need to produce?**

> **Against what threshold or reference rate should I interpret what is happening?**

This is not a demand for numerical precision in every decision. The relevant threshold may be quantitative, qualitative, contextual, negotiated, or bounded by genuine guardrails.

## Existing canon independently reinforced by the source

The private source also supplies additional evidence for already-canonical Book 6 principles without creating duplicate doctrine:

- **favorable outcome does not prove a decision was well earned** — a later strong performance outcome should not be used to retroactively validate an underexamined hiring process;
- **option quality is not the same as person–option fit** — an externally respectable career path may still be a poor fit for a particular person;
- **decision authority and development of judgment are distinct** — providing more for another person can create legitimate questions about ownership, learning, appreciation, and agency;
- **consider both the cost of waiting and the cost of rushing** — some opportunities change or decay while a person continues to deliberate.

These are supporting source functions only. The governing conceptual authority remains the existing canon controls.

## Hindsight and outcome guardrail

Do not convert a favorable later outcome into proof that an earlier choice was well reasoned.

Do not convert later success into evidence that every ignored concern was irrelevant.

Do not convert later difficulty into proof that persistence was mistaken.

At each decision point, evaluate what was knowable then, what evidence actually mattered, what threshold the decision required, and what materially new evidence later action produced.

## Architecture implication

This refinement strengthens the provisional architecture without changing it.

### Movement III — Enough

- when continued effort is improving the decision versus merely extending commitment;
- whether the method should change while the objective remains;
- what threshold makes accumulated evidence sufficient to continue, revise, or stop.

### Movement V — Decide Without Certainty

- expected failure rates versus evidence that a strategy is actually failing;
- outcomes as later evidence without hindsight rewriting;
- continuation as a new decision rather than automatic execution of an old one.
