Underlying editorial movement:

**premature certainty → wider perspective → sufficiency → authority → action under uncertainty → learning**

## Movement I — The Easy Answer

**Status:** STRONG.

Editorial job: diagnose decision-as-avoidance.

The movement is supported by both premature-no and premature-yes evidence. The easy-yes mirror must appear early enough that the book cannot be read as “say yes more.”

Boundary with Movement II:

- Movement I: **Am I using the answer to stop the work?**
- Movement II: **What additional perspective or decomposition does the work require?**

**Decision:** retain.

## Movement II — See More Before You Decide

**Status:** VERY STRONG.

Editorial job: show how a decision changes when the field of view widens.

Recurring mechanisms include:

- requirement versus assumption;
- objective versus implementation;
- constraint versus conclusion;
- prototype versus production;
- fact versus interpretation;
- distributed expertise;
- general expertise versus better-situated knowledge;
- listening before no.

Risk: becoming a catalog of “consider more perspectives.”

Countermeasure: each story must show what changed in the decision when something new became visible.

Natural hinge into Movement III:

> **More can always be seen. When has enough been seen?**

**Decision:** retain.

## Movement III — Enough

**Status:** STRONG / LIKELY INTELLECTUAL CENTER.

Editorial job: define decision sufficiency without pretending there is one universal threshold.

Key questions:

> **When is enough, enough?**

> **Enough for what, and according to whose priorities?**

This movement carries:

- values and criteria;
- visible tradeoffs;
- persistence versus stopping;
- negotiated sufficiency;
- changing thresholds with an account;
- earned efficiency;
- bounded next steps;
- incremental confidence.

Key guardrail:

> **A justified next step earns only that step.**

Risk: abstraction.

Countermeasure: use a small number of high-stakes narrative carriers rather than one chapter per concept.

The paired private-source cases remain especially useful as an echo structure:

**NO → inquiry → understanding → integration**

then later:

**YES / commitment → persistence → evidence → letting go**

**Decision:** retain.

## Movement IV — Whose Decision?

**Status:** STRONG / DISTINCTIVE.

Editorial job: separate “what would I choose?” from “is this mine to decide?”

This movement carries:

- decision authority;
- intrinsic agency versus delegated responsibility;
- experience as map rather than script;
- situated knowledge;
- demonstrated judgment;
- delegated latitude;
- trust as evidence;
- handoff and successor autonomy;
- intervention versus substitution.

Key canon:

> **Decision authority does not imply informational completeness.**

> **The work of earning a decision can be distributed even when final decision authority is not.**

Primary contradiction still needed:

A case in which delegated latitude appropriately had to be narrowed or intervention became necessary.

**Decision:** retain.

## Movement V — Decide Without Certainty

**Status:** CONCEPTUALLY STRONG / COUNTEREXAMPLE BALANCE STILL DEVELOPING.

Editorial job: resolve the false promise that sufficient inquiry eliminates uncertainty.

Boundary with Movement III:

- Movement III: **When has pre-decision inquiry become sufficient?**
- Movement V: **What does responsible action look like when sufficient still leaves uncertainty?**

This movement carries:

- irreducible uncertainty;
- bounded commitment;
- action that generates evidence;
- optionality;
- decision quality versus outcome quality;
- hindsight;
- continuation and stopping as new decisions;
- handoff risk;
- post-decision learning.

Current priority gap:

- weak or narrow process with favorable outcome;
- bounded experiment that creates harmful commitment creep;
- “we cannot know until we act” used to rationalize reckless action;
- accumulated trust that becomes stale.

A private family-source lead was reviewed against this gap. Current evidence does not support a clean weak-process / lucky-outcome classification. It instead produced the canonical person–option fit refinement in `24_CANON_2026-09-30_PERSON_OPTION_FIT_AND_TRUSTED_JUDGMENT.md`: a trusted advisor may understand the external quality of an option while the chooser's person-option fit remains less explored. The lucky-outcome gap therefore remains open.

**Decision:** retain and prioritize contradiction mining.

## Sequence test

Current sequence:

**I → II → III → IV → V**

remains preferred.

Reason:

- I establishes the failure mode.
- II opens the field.
- III establishes sufficiency.
- IV asks whether the decision belongs to the person who has reached a view.
- V addresses residual uncertainty, outcome learning, and revision.

Moving authority earlier was considered and is not currently stronger editorially.

## Overlap test

### I versus II

Distinct if:

- I remains about **avoidance / premature closure**;
- II remains about **what additional seeing and decomposition require**.

### II versus III

Strong natural hinge:

**See more → when is it enough?**

### III versus V

Both are necessary.

- III = sufficiency before commitment.
- V = uncertainty that remains after sufficiency.

### IV versus V

Handoff cases can span both. Keep:

- IV centered on **ownership / authority**;
- V centered on **uncertainty / outcomes / deciding again**.

## Additional-movement tests

### Add “After the Decision” as Movement VI?

**No.**

Continuation, stopping, learning, and reopening naturally belong inside Movement V.

### Make values a separate movement?

**No.**

Values give “enough” its meaning and belong primarily in Movement III.

### Make trust a separate movement?

**No.**

Trust is decision-relevant evidence and a mechanism across sufficiency, authority, and uncertainty.
