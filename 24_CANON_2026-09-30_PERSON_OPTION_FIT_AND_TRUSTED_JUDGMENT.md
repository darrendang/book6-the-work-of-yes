## Existing spine preserved

> **What does someone need to see—or understand—before they have earned enough confidence to decide?**

> **Earn the decision.**

This refinement sharpens what “understand” may require when a choice concerns a particular person rather than an option in the abstract.

## Canonical distinction 1 — option quality is not person–option fit

An option can be objectively strong, prestigious, safe, efficient, or otherwise attractive while still being an imperfect fit for a particular person.

Therefore:

> **Option quality is not the same as person–option fit.**

Relevant fit evidence may include preferences, temperament, learning style, aspirations, desired mode of work, environment, consequence ownership, and other context specific to the chooser.

This does not imply that every preference should control or that external quality is unimportant.

## Canonical distinction 2 — trusted judgment can carry evidence without supplying self-knowledge

A trusted advisor may know more about the external option than the chooser and may legitimately reduce how much external inquiry must be repeated.

But:

> **Trusted judgment can carry relevant external evidence; it does not eliminate the chooser’s need for self-inquiry.**

Another person can help evaluate the option.

They cannot automatically know the chooser’s evolving preferences, identity, aspirations, or fit better than the chooser eventually may.

This extends the existing canon:

> **The work of earning a decision can be distributed even when final decision authority is not.**

Distributed decision work can include trusted research and judgment. It does not make every dimension of the decision transferable.

## Canonical distinction 3 — later self-knowledge is new evidence, not proof of an earlier mistake

Some evidence about fit may become visible only after living the choice.

Therefore later self-knowledge may legitimately change how a prior decision is interpreted.

It does **not** automatically prove:

- that the original decision was unreasonable;
- that another option would have been better;
- that the later insight was available at the original decision point;
- or that the historical chooser should have known what only later experience revealed.

This preserves the existing hindsight guardrail:

> **An earned decision does not guarantee a good outcome.**

and adds the complementary point that later learning can refine future judgment without rewriting the past.

## Adopted inquiry

> **Have we evaluated how good the option is—or how good the option is for this person?**

Companion questions:

> **What must I understand about the person making the choice—not just the choice itself?**

> **When can another person’s well-earned judgment legitimately reduce inquiry that does not need to be repeated?**

> **When does reliance on a trusted advisor become borrowed certainty rather than earned efficiency?**

> **What evidence about fit can only emerge after living the choice?**

These are inquiry lenses, not a mandatory checklist.

## Relationship to decision authority

This refinement strengthens the distinction between informed advice and decision ownership.

A more experienced or better-informed advisor may contribute substantial evidence without thereby becoming the sole authority on the chooser’s fit.

Conversely, preserving the chooser’s agency does not require ignoring relevant expertise.

The work is to distinguish:

- what the advisor knows about the external option;
- what the chooser knows about the particular context and self;
- what remains unknown;
- who owns the consequence;
- and whether the decision-maker has enough judgment and authority to act.

## Architecture implication

This refinement naturally spans three current provisional movements:

### Movement II — See More Before You Decide

Widen perspective from the quality of the option to the quality of the fit.

### Movement IV — Whose Decision?

Distinguish informed advice from ownership of the choice and development of the chooser’s own judgment.

### Movement V — Decide Without Certainty

Acknowledge that some fit evidence can emerge only after living the decision.

This does not alter or lock the current five-movement architecture.

## Hindsight and counterfactual guardrail

Do not use this canon to claim that:

- a different school, career, relationship, or path would have been better;
- a high-quality option was therefore a bad choice;
- an advisor’s informed recommendation was necessarily wrong;
- or later dissatisfaction or mismatch invalidates the original decision process.

The transferable lesson is about the distinction among external quality, personal fit, trusted evidence, self-inquiry, and later learning.
