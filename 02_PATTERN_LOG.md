# Book 6 — Pattern Log

The Book 6 project remains exploratory, but the September 16, 2026 voice conversation established a canonical conceptual spine. Chapter architecture remains uncommitted.

## Canonical conceptual spine

### 1. The book is about decision-making

Yes and no are outcomes. The deeper subject is how a responsible person reaches a consequential decision without stopping inquiry too early or continuing inquiry too long.

### 2. Earn the decision

The central responsibility is to do enough work that the decision itself is earned.

An earned decision is supported by sufficient perspective, inquiry, imagination, evidence, and judgment to act with confidence in either direction.

### 3. Perspective matters before certainty

A decision can fail because the field of view is too narrow. Wider perspective may reveal:
- a hidden assumption;
- a different architecture;
- a second-order effect;
- a neglected stakeholder;
- a reason to persist;
- or a reason to stop.

The goal is not endless analysis. It is enough perspective to avoid premature certainty.

### 4. Yin-yang decision tensions

The book should preserve and explore paired tensions rather than resolving them through a universal rule:

- yes ↔ no
- persistence ↔ letting go
- optimism ↔ realism
- possibility ↔ constraint
- speed ↔ sufficient inquiry
- confidence ↔ humility
- courage to continue ↔ courage to stop

Neither side is inherently correct.

### 5. Premature certainty is a central failure mode

An easy no can avoid the work of possibility.

An easy yes can avoid the work of judgment.

Both are forms of premature certainty.

### 6. Persistence and stopping both require judgment

Persistence without judgment can become stubbornness.

Judgment without sufficient persistence can become premature surrender.

Stopping may be responsible leadership when continued effort would waste resources, violate guardrails, or sacrifice people to save an objective that should be abandoned.

## Canonical candidate decision movement

Objective → initial perspective → apparent constraint or opportunity → easy answer available → widen perspective → distinguish requirement from assumption → generate and test alternatives → examine second-order effects and guardrails → decide whether further inquiry is still valuable → commit → earned decision.

The endpoint may be:
- earned yes;
- earned no;
- deliberate deferral when more evidence is genuinely required.

## Earlier patterns retained for continued testing

- Premature no
- Institutional self-protection
- Local optimization versus responsibility for the whole
- Constraint versus requirement
- Guardrail versus roadblock
- Requirement versus assumption
- Reframing the objective
- Alternative architecture
- Constructive constraint
- Stewardship under constraint

These remain useful hypotheses and should continue to be tested against independent examples rather than treated as a finished framework.
