## Approved contribution — substantial effort and the required work

Distinguish **doing substantial work** from **doing the work the decision actually requires**.

Where material, understanding the decision includes examining the role, allocation of effort, opportunity cost, and responsibility to others. Progress on a selected problem does not by itself settle whether personal involvement, staffing, coaching, consultation, continued inquiry, or intervention is the appropriate response.

This develops the existing sufficiency, visible-tradeoff, and decision-authority controls. It does not equate greater effort with better judgment or technical immersion with avoidance.

## Approved inquiry — work that leaves a responsibility unexamined

> **Can substantial effort on an interesting problem leave the harder responsibility unexamined?**

This is an approved inquiry to test against evidence. It is not a universal finding about motives or proof that any particular source participant intentionally avoided responsibility.

Preserve the counterweight from the reviewed dialogue: hands-on inquiry may build the understanding needed to evaluate advice, appreciate alternatives, coach well, or intervene responsibly. The interview does not establish that earlier delegation would have produced equivalent learning or a better overall outcome.
