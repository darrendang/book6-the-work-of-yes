## Current canonical intellectual spine

Book 6 is fundamentally about **decision-making**.

### Governing question

> **What does someone need to see—or understand—before they have earned enough confidence to decide?**

### Core principle

> **Earn the decision.**

Yes and no are outcomes. The work is to reach a decision with enough perspective, inquiry, imagination, evidence, and judgment to act with justified confidence in either direction.

### The Avoidance Principle

> **The easy no avoids the work of possibility.**

> **The easy yes avoids the work of judgment.**

Both can be forms of **premature certainty**.

> **Do not use a decision to avoid the work the decision requires.**

This preserves the original seed premise — that it is easy to say no and harder to find a workable path toward yes — while adding the necessary counterweight: it can also be dangerous to work too hard toward yes when stopping is the responsible choice.

## Canonical decision tensions

The book should preserve the yin-yang character of responsible decision-making rather than privileging either side automatically:

- yes ↔ no
- persistence ↔ letting go
- optimism ↔ realism
- possibility ↔ constraint
- speed ↔ sufficient inquiry
- confidence ↔ humility
- courage to continue ↔ courage to stop

Neither side is inherently correct. Perspective and judgment determine which response is earned.
