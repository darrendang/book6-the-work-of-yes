## Existing spine preserved

> **What does someone need to see—or understand—before they have earned enough confidence to decide?**

> **Earn the decision.**

This refinement strengthens the book's contradiction side: a good result can follow weak judgment, a small beginning can grow into an unearned larger commitment, and previously earned trust can become less relevant when the decision environment changes.

## Canonical finding 1 — favorable outcomes do not validate weak process

The existing Book 6 canon already establishes:

> **An earned decision does not guarantee a good outcome.**

The mirror is now explicit:

> **A favorable outcome does not prove that the decision was well earned.**

Luck, timing, external conditions, or other factors can produce a good result even when the inquiry, evidence, or judgment was under-developed.

Therefore outcome quality must not be used as a substitute for process quality in either direction.

Adopted inquiry:

> **If the outcome had gone the other way, would I still defend the decision process?**

This is an audit question, not a demand for perfect foresight.

## Canonical finding 2 — size the commitment to the confidence earned

Where a decision can reasonably be staged or bounded, the size of the commitment should reflect the confidence actually earned.

> **A decision can be too large for the confidence that has actually been earned.**

This does not mean every consequential choice can be made small or reversible.

It means the decision-maker should distinguish:

- confidence in the objective;
- confidence in the particular path;
- confidence in the people or assumptions involved;
- and the amount of downside being accepted.

A larger commitment may require stronger evidence, stronger guardrails, greater downside capacity, or an explicit decision to carry the additional uncertainty.

## Canonical finding 3 — a bounded step must stay bounded

The prior canon states:

> **A justified next step earns only that step.**

This source sharpens the implication:

> **A bounded step is only bounded if the next commitment must be earned again.**

An experiment, pilot, trial, or initial commitment can begin small and still become commitment creep if later resources, time, identity, money, or authority are added without fresh judgment.

The boundary is not the size of the first step alone.

The boundary must survive the transition to the next one.

Adopted inquiry:

> **What would have to become true before I commit more?**

## Canonical finding 4 — irreducible uncertainty is not a license for unbounded action

Some evidence can only exist after action.

That remains an important Book 6 truth.

But:

> **“We cannot know until we act” does not by itself justify how much we risk.**

When uncertainty is genuinely irreducible, responsible action may still require attention to:

- downside capacity;
- guardrails;
- reversibility or exit options;
- review conditions;
- who owns the consequence;
- what evidence the action is intended to generate;
- and what later evidence would justify continuation, escalation, adjustment, or stopping.

This does not create a universal pilot method. Some decisions are inherently indivisible.

## Canonical finding 5 — trust as evidence is domain-specific and revisable

Book 6 already recognizes:

> **Trust can become evidence.**

This refinement adds the counterweight:

> **Evidence of competence is contextual.**

Past performance can justify confidence only to the extent that the prior evidence remains relevant to the current role, stakes, responsibilities, and decision environment.

Therefore:

> **Previously earned trust should remain responsive to new relevant evidence.**

In delegated responsibility, continued contrary evidence can justify narrowing latitude, additional support, reassignment, or intervention.

This does not mean trust should be withdrawn at the first mistake or that people cannot grow into new roles.

The question is whether prior evidence still predicts the judgment the current responsibility requires.

Adopted inquiry:

> **Is the trust I am relying on supported by evidence that still applies here?**

## Canonical finding 6 — ongoing commitments may require explicit review conditions

For ongoing, revisable commitments whose assumptions can materially change, part of earning the decision may be knowing what would cause the decision to be revisited.

Adopted inquiry:

> **What materially new evidence would cause me to reconsider?**

A review condition is not a promise to reverse course automatically.

Its purpose is to prevent emotion, sunk cost, habit, or a vague “long-term” posture from becoming substitutes for renewed judgment.

This principle is contextual. One-time or irreversible choices may not permit meaningful review after commitment.

## Retained developmental lead — defaults deserve scrutiny

The voice conversation also surfaced a possible pattern:

> **A default is not automatically an earned decision.**

This remains **DEVELOPMENTAL / NOT CANONIZED BY THIS CONTROL**.

A default can be entirely reasonable. The open question is when default behavior prevents the decision-maker from comparing relevant horizons, alternatives, or consequences.

Continue testing before promotion.
